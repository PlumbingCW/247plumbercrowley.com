Sewage or Water Backing Up Right Now: First Ten Minutes

A backup is wastewater with nowhere to go. These moves limit the damage until a licensed plumber is on the way.

  1. Stop adding water.

    Do not flush, run the washer, or start the dishwasher. Every gallon you add goes to the lowest open drain, usually a tub or shower on the ground floor.

  2. Keep people and pets clear.

    Backed-up drain water carries bacteria. Keep children and pets out, wear rubber gloves and boots if you must step in it, and wash your hands afterward.

  3. Skip the chemical drain opener.

    Do not pour bleach or caustic drain cleaner into a backup. It sits in the standing water, burns skin, and makes the job unsafe for the plumber who has to open the line.

  4. Check whether more than one drain is slow.

    If only the kitchen sink is stuck, the clog is local. If the toilet gurgles when you run the shower, or a floor drain bubbles, the blockage is in the main line. Tell us which drains are involved when you call.

  5. Call for same-day or after-hours help.

    Dispatch answers at any hour, and a backup at 2 a.m. gets the same attention as one at noon. Call (817) 601-6266 and we will line up the next available licensed plumber.

Clogs by Fixture: Kitchen, Shower, Tub and Main Line

Where the water backs up tells us a lot. We start with the fixture, then follow the pipe toward the main line until the blockage is found.

Drain work is held to the venting and trap standards in the plumbing code, and Crowley uses the 2021 International Plumbing Code. That standard is why a clean drain still needs a properly vented trap to keep working.

Kitchen sink clogs

Kitchen drains are narrow, usually 1.5 inch pipe, and grease is the main culprit. Cooking fat runs through the trap as liquid, cools, and coats the pipe wall until only a pencil-sized opening is left. Coffee grounds, rice and eggshells then catch on that coating.

We run a drum machine with a cutting head to scrape the wall clean instead of poking a hole through the middle. If your disposal is part of the problem, we also look at garbage disposal repair before closing up.

Shower and tub drains

Hair is the usual blockage here, bound together with soap scum. Crowley water runs 6 to 9 grains per gallon of hardness, and hard water leaves a chalky film that gives hair something to grip. A small cable with a barbed tip pulls the mass out in one piece.

If a tub drains fine but a shower on the same branch is slow, the clog is farther down the line, past the point where the two join. We run the cable from the nearest accessible cleanout instead of fighting through the trap.

Main line blockages

The main line is the 3 or 4 inch pipe that carries everything from the house toward the street connection. When it clogs, the lowest fixture shows it first, often a ground-floor tub or a floor drain.

We open the cleanout, run a larger cable, and then send a camera to see what caused it. If the camera shows roots or a crushed section, we talk through sewer line cleaning or sewer line repair with you before any work starts.

Slow drains that are not fully stopped

A slow drain is an early warning. Water that takes ten seconds longer to leave the sink means the pipe is already narrowing. Clearing it now is a short visit, while waiting turns it into a full backup on a holiday weekend.

Snaking, Cabling and Jetting: What Each Does

People use these words loosely, so here is how they differ in practice.

Hand snake

A hand-cranked auger for a clog close to the fixture. Good for a trap or the first few feet of a branch line, not for anything deeper.

Drum machine cable

A motorized cable in sizes from 1/4 inch to 3/4 inch. It spins a cutting head through grease, hair, paper and light roots.

Camera inspection

A flexible camera on a push rod shows the pipe from inside. We use it to confirm the clog is gone and to spot cracks, offsets or a low spot holding water.

Hydro-jetting

Water at high pressure scours the full pipe wall. It is the choice for heavy grease, scale and repeat clogs, and we cover it on the hydro-jetting page.

Cleanout access

A capped fitting that gives a straight path into the main line. If your home lacks a usable one, we can often add one so future service is faster and cleaner.

Pipe repair

When the camera shows damage, cleaning only buys time. Spot repair or replacement of the failed section is the honest fix.

What You Can Try First, and When to Stop

A plunger and a few minutes can save a service call on a simple clog. Pushing past that point can make things worse.

Safe to try yourself

  • Use a flat-bottom cup plunger on a sink or tub with the overflow plugged by a wet rag.
  • Use a flange plunger on a toilet and give it a dozen steady strokes.
  • Remove the hair stopper and pull out what you can see by hand.
  • Flush a slow kitchen drain with a kettle of hot, not boiling, water and dish soap.

Call a plumber when

  • More than one fixture is slow or gurgling.
  • Water or sewage comes up in a tub, shower or floor drain.
  • You smell sewer gas after plunging.
  • The clog returns within days or weeks.
  • A store-bought auger gets stuck or the cable will not advance.

Habits That Keep Drains Running

Most clogs we clear were made slowly over months. A few habits stretch the time between visits.

  • Scrape pans and plates into the trash, and let used cooking grease cool in a can instead of the sink.
  • Use a hair catcher in every shower and tub drain, and empty it after each use.
  • Flush only toilet paper and human waste. Wipes sold as flushable still catch on roots and rough pipe joints.
  • Run hot water for 30 seconds after washing greasy dishes to carry residue past the trap.
  • Pour a kettle of hot water and a squirt of dish soap down the kitchen drain once a month.
  • Keep the cleanout cap clear of mulch, soil and landscaping so it can be found fast.

If you do all of that and a drain still slows, the pipe itself may have a low spot, a root intrusion or scale buildup. That is a camera job, and a short visit usually settles it.
