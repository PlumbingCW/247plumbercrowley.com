The First 10 Minutes of a Plumbing Emergency

What you do before help arrives often decides whether you replace a pipe or replace a floor. These five moves cover most household emergencies.

  1. Find and close the main water shutoff.

    Most Crowley homes have a main valve near the water heater, in the garage, or at the meter box near the street. A quarter-turn ball valve closes with a half turn of the lever, while an older gate valve needs several turns clockwise. If you cannot find it or it will not move, do not force it with a wrench.

  2. Cut power and gas to anything wet.

    If water is near outlets, a panel, or a power strip, keep everyone away and switch off the breaker only if you can reach it while standing on dry floor. For a leaking water heater, turn the thermostat to off or pilot, shut the cold supply on top of the tank, and leave the gas valve alone if you smell gas.

  3. Stop using drains and fixtures.

    If a toilet or floor drain is backing up, do not flush, run the washer, or start the dishwasher. Every gallon you add has nowhere to go. Keep kids and pets out of the area and skip bleach or chemical drain openers.

  4. Move valuables and start soaking up water.

    Lift rugs, electronics and cardboard boxes off the floor. Use towels and a wet-dry vacuum on standing water, and open a window if the room feels damp. Take photos of the damage first, because your insurance carrier will ask for them.

  5. Call, then keep the line open.

    Call (866) 310-6010 and tell the dispatcher what you shut off and what you can still see. For a water main break in the street or a city sewer backup, the City of Crowley lists Police Dispatch at 817-297-2276 for after-hours reports, and you should also call us for the plumbing inside your property line.

Local Plumbing Reality in a Fast-Growing Town

Crowley grew 40.8 percent between the 2010 and 2020 census counts, from 12,838 people to 18,070, so many homes here are newer than the older streets around the railroad downtown. That mix matters. A recent subdivision such as Sendero Oaks, Mayfair or Lasater Ranch brings different questions than an older house near the original depot, and we never assume a pipe material from the neighborhood name alone.

The practical tie-back is a pipe check on the first visit. Homes built before the mid-1990s may still have polybutylene or galvanized supply lines, and the plumber should look for them regardless of which development you live in. Ask the plumber what pipe they see at the water heater and the meter, because that tells you more than a street name.

Planned Work Between the Emergencies

Not every call is a flood. Some of our best work happens on an ordinary weekday, when you book a water heater installation before the old tank fails, or schedule a sewer camera inspection before buying a house. A tank that is 8 to 12 years old is worth planning around, and a camera run shows roots or sags in a line before they turn into a backup.

If your faucets spit rust-colored water or the same drain clogs every few months, the cheap fix is rarely the lasting one. Repeat clogs may call for hydro jetting, and old supply lines that keep springing leaks may justify whole house repiping. We lay out both options and the trade-offs so you decide with real information.

How to Check Any Plumber in Texas

The Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners (TSBPE) licenses plumbers in this state. Ask any company for its Responsible Master Plumber, then look that license up in the board's Public License Search, where Master Plumber numbers start with M. The same search shows whether a Certificate of Insurance is on file.

Do this for us and for anyone else you call. A short phone check beats a long argument after a bad repair, and an honest company will not mind. Our about page explains how our dispatch works, and our contact page lists the address and hours.
