Water Is Running Somewhere You Cannot See: What to Do First

Hidden leaks do their worst damage in the first few hours. These three moves cost nothing and often save the drywall, the flooring, and the water bill.

  1. Stop the water at the main.

    Find the main shutoff valve for the house. A gate valve takes several full turns clockwise, while a ball valve (a lever handle) needs a quarter turn so the handle sits crosswise to the pipe. If the valve will not move or the leak is outside, leave it alone and call us.

  2. Protect people and electronics.

    Water and electricity do not mix. If water is dripping through a ceiling light, near a panel, or around outlets, stay clear and switch off that breaker only if the panel and floor around it are completely dry.

  3. Take two minutes of notes.

    Write down where you see stains, damp spots, or hear hissing, and snap a photo of the water meter dial. Those details shorten the search because a plumber can start at the wet zone instead of the whole house. Then call (817) 601-6266 and we will line up a plumber.

The Meter Test: Proving a Hidden Leak in 20 Minutes

Your water meter is the most honest witness in the house. It counts every gallon that passes, including the ones that vanish into a slab or a yard. If it moves when nothing is running, water is leaking somewhere on your side of the meter.

The test is simple, and it works at any hour. It also explains most of the high-bill calls we get in Crowley, where a small leak can run for weeks before anyone notices.

Finding the leak is only half the job, because the repair has to meet the plumbing code standard too. Crowley has adopted the 2021 International Plumbing Code, and any permit questions go to the Community Development office.

How to run it

Turn off every faucet, the ice maker, the dishwasher, and the washing machine. Open the meter box lid and look for the small leak indicator, a tiny triangle or star dial that spins when even a trickle moves through. Note the position of the sweep hand or the last digit, wait 15 to 30 minutes with the water off, and look again.

If the indicator is still turning or the number has crept up, you have a leak. If it holds perfectly still, your leak is probably not constant, and the problem may be a toilet flapper or a pressure-triggered leak that only shows when a fixture cycles.

What a moving meter tells you

Close the main valve at the house and check the meter again. If the dial stops, the leak is inside the house or on the house side of that valve. If the dial keeps spinning with the valve closed, the loss is between the meter and the shutoff, which points to the service line.

For the meter itself, the box, or where the city side of the line ends, call City of Crowley Public Works at 817-297-2201. Ask them where the city side ends and bring up any leak you can see at the meter, since we cannot assume who maintains what.

Why Crowley Water and Soil Make Leaks Hard to Catch

The City of Crowley's 2025 Water Quality Report lists pH between 7.8 and 8.5, which is mildly alkaline. That is not an acidic, pipe-eating profile, so when we find a pinhole in copper here we also look at water velocity, a poor joint, or where the pipe touches soil or concrete. The point for you is that a leak is usually a local failure, not a sign every pipe in the house is about to go.

The ground matters too. In this part of North Texas the clay soil swells when wet and shrinks when dry, which can stress pipes that pass through or under a slab. That movement is a common reason a line that held for years suddenly weeps, and it is why we test the pipe itself instead of guessing from the stain.

Where Hidden Leaks Live in a Home

Each location needs a different method. Here is how we think about the six places callers describe most.

Under the slab

Slab leaks hide in lines running through concrete, so we listen with ground microphones and confirm with thermal imaging. A confirmed slab leak moves to slab leak repair, where you choose between rerouting and breaking the floor.

Service line

The pipe from the meter to the house can leak in the yard, with a wet strip or sunken spot as the clue. We pressure-test it and trace its path with a locator before any digging starts. Repairs fall under water service line repair.

Ceiling and walls

Stains and soft drywall can come from a supply line, a drain, or a tub overflow. We probe with a moisture meter, scan with thermal imaging, and open a small inspection hole only when we know where to cut.

Crawlspace and pier-and-beam

Homes with a crawlspace let us inspect lines from below. We look for dripping joints, wet insulation, and soil that stays damp, and we do not ask you to crawl in yourself.

Irrigation

A stuck zone valve or cracked lateral can drain gallons into a yard all day and night. Crowley's local IPC amendment, section 608.17.5, does not allow a valve downstream of an atmospheric vacuum breaker on lawn irrigation, so we check how yours is plumbed.

Pools and spas

Pool water loss can be evaporation or a crack in a return line. We pressure-test the plumbing lines in sections to find which one is losing water, and we leave the pool shell to a pool specialist.

How We Find a Leak Without Tearing Up the House

A good leak detection visit follows a sequence, and every step rules something out. We confirm the leak with the meter and a pressure gauge, isolate which line is losing water, and then pinpoint the spot. Skipping the sequence is how people end up with three holes in a wall.

We bring several tools because no single one solves every leak. Each is matched to the pipe and the surroundings.

Pressure testing and isolation

We close off sections of the system and watch a gauge. A needle that falls on the hot side only points to the hot line, and a drop on the cold side narrows it to a different run. Isolating lines this way can cut a search from an entire house to a single wall or a section of floor.

Acoustic and thermal tools

A ground microphone amplifies the hiss of pressurized water as it escapes. A thermal imaging camera reads temperature differences on floors and walls, which is useful for hot water leaks in a slab. We cross-check both with a moisture meter before marking anything.

Tracer gas and camera work

For stubborn leaks we can fill a line with a harmless tracer gas and sniff the surface with a sensor. If the suspect line is a drain, a sewer camera inspection, which you can read about under sewer inspection, shows cracks, root entry, or a bellied pipe from the inside.

What You Can Check Yourself and When to Call

A few checks cost nothing and rule out the easy culprits. Others need equipment and a plumber.

Safe for you to do

  • Run the meter test and write down the numbers.
  • Look under sinks for damp cabinets or dripping stops.
  • Drop food coloring into the toilet tank and watch the bowl for 15 minutes.
  • Check the water heater pan and the area around the tank.
  • Inspect hose bibbs and irrigation valve boxes for standing water.

Time to call a plumber

  • The meter runs with the main valve closed.
  • A floor feels warm or you hear hissing in a wall.
  • Water stains appear and spread within a day.
  • Your bill doubled and the toilets and fixtures check out.
  • You see foundation cracks near a wet area, which needs a plumber and possibly a foundation specialist.
