From Brambleton Station to a City of 13,955

Settlement here began around 1860 as Brambleton Station and Forest Hill Village. By 1905, Forest Hill Drive and Old Mansfield Road were the two main roads, and the village later incorporated in 1946 and became a city in 1949. Those dates describe the community, not any one house, and we never assume a pipe type from a date alone.

What the longer history does tell you is that Forest Hill has had more time for repairs, additions and patchwork. A home that has been remodeled twice may carry three kinds of supply pipe. Homes built before the mid-1990s may have galvanized steel, polybutylene or cast iron drains, and a quick inspection beats a guess.

Forest Hill sets its own permit and inspection rules, so ask the city which edition of the plumbing code it enforces before a larger job. Our licensed plumbers work to the code standard in force where the house stands.

Leak Detection in an Older, Dense City

A hidden leak shows up first as a water bill that climbs, a damp spot on a slab, or the sound of running water when nothing is on. Check by turning off every fixture and watching the meter dial for movement. If it turns, water is going somewhere, and leak detection finds the spot with acoustic gear and pressure testing instead of opening floors at random.

North Texas clay swells and shrinks with the seasons, and slab foundations can shift enough to stress a buried line. If the leak is under the floor, see our slab leak repair page for repair paths. A leak under a street-side yard may need a look at the water line from meter to house.

Before We Arrive in Forest Hill

A failed tank or a burst line is easier to handle if a few things are done before the truck pulls up.

Find the main and close it.

The main shutoff is usually where the line enters the house or at the meter. Turn it until water stops. If it is stuck, leave it and tell the dispatcher.

Power down the heater.

Kill the breaker or gas control on a leaking heater, and shut its cold inlet. Never relight a pilot near a leak. A gas smell means leave and call the gas utility or 911.

Protect what is on the floor.

Lift rugs, move electronics and open cabinets under the sink to let air in. Keep clear of wet outlets and the panel.

What We Check When a House Has Had Many Owners

On older homes a pipe check comes before the replacement quote.

  • Supply lines.We look for galvanized steel, gray polybutylene and mixed copper and PEX joints.
  • Drain stack.Cast iron drains can crack or corrode, which shows as slow drains or odor.
  • Pressure.A gauge on a hose bib shows whether a regulator is failing and stressing fittings.
  • Water heater.We record the age, the anode rod, the T&P valve and whether an expansion tank is present.

Replacing a Water Heater Instead of Patching It

When a tank leaks from the seam, nothing seals the steel, and water heater replacement is the honest fix. Permit rules vary, so ask the Forest Hill building office or tell us and we will help you ask. Tankless units last around 20 years but need venting and gas checks.

If the tank is still sound, the cost can sit in a single part, so look at water heater repair first. We price both choices on the phone. To compare nearby coverage, see Burleson and Everman.
