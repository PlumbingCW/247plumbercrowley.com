The Water Heater Is Usually the First Big Item

A tank water heater typically lasts around 8 to 12 years, and a tankless unit around 20. We have not verified when each Crescent Springs Ranch home was built, so check the label on your own tank for the manufacture date. A tank near the end of that range is worth planning for before it fails on a cold morning.

The key parts are the anode rod, the temperature and pressure relief valve and, in many homes, an expansion tank. If the T&P valve drips or the tank rumbles, call before it becomes a puddle. Water heater replacement is easiest when the old tank is still working.

Kitchen and Bath Fixtures That Wear Out

A toilet that runs or rocks

A worn flapper or fill valve wastes water quietly. A rocking base needs the wax ring or flange checked. Toilet repair usually fixes it in one visit.

A disposal that hums but will not spin

A jam is common and often clearable. A leak from the housing usually means replacement. See garbage disposal repair for the signs.

A burst pipe after a hard freeze

A hard freeze is occasional in North Texas but real, and the 2021 winter storm burst pipes across the region. Know where your main shutoff is, and see burst pipe repair if one lets go.

Questions to Ask Before You Approve a Replacement

  • What size tank fits my household, and why?
  • Will the new unit need venting or a new expansion tank?
  • Does the work meet the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Crowley has adopted, and who pulls the permit?

Any reasonable plumber can answer these on the phone. If the answers are vague, get a second opinion.
