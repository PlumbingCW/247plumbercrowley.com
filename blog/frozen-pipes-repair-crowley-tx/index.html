Frozen Pipes in Crowley, TX: Thaw, Repair and Prevent Bursts

Open the tap, warm the pipe with a hair dryer, and never use a flame. That is the safe thaw for a frozen pipe in a Crowley, TX home. Shut the main valve first if you see any sign of a split.

Thaw a Frozen Pipe the Safe Way

Do these in order. Stop and call a plumber the moment you find water you cannot stop.

  1. Locate the main shutoff.

    Find it before you touch anything. It is often at the meter, at the house wall, or in a garage. Turn it off if you suspect a crack, because ice can plug a split pipe and hide it until the thaw.

  2. Open the faucet.

    Open the tap on the frozen line, both hot and cold if both are slow. Melting ice needs a place to push water and steam out, and an open tap relieves pressure.

  3. Apply gentle heat.

    Use a hair dryer, a heating pad or warm towels. Start at the faucet end and work toward the blockage so meltwater has an exit. Keep any cord and dryer away from standing water.

  4. Check every joint.

    Look and feel for wet spots, bulges and hairline splits along the pipe. Run your hand under cabinets, since a split often leaks only when the line thaws.

  5. Run the water slowly.

    Once flow returns, open the faucet slowly and watch for drips at fittings. If anything weeps, shut the valve and make the call.

Never Thaw With an Open Flame

Fire and scorched pipes

A torch, a lighter or a space heater aimed at a pipe can ignite insulation, framing or the wall behind it. Copper conducts heat fast, so a torch can also melt solder or overheat the joint. Plastic pipe can deform. Stay with warm air, warm water and a heating pad.

Why Pipes Freeze and Then Burst

Water expands about nine percent as it turns to ice. The pipe does not usually split at the ice plug itself. The split forms where trapped water between the plug and a closed faucet gets squeezed by pressure, so the pipe fails along its weakest stretch.

Pipes in attics, exterior walls, crawlspaces, garages and under mobile-home skirts freeze first. So do supply lines to outdoor spigots and washing machine boxes on exterior walls. A hard freeze is occasional in North Texas, but the 2021 winter storm burst pipes across the region, and homes that rarely see freezing weather are often the least prepared.

Insulation guidance from the plumbing code and the manufacturer is the standard to follow before the next freeze. A pipe that has split needs a licensed repair, not tape.

Homeowner Fix or Plumber Call

Thawing a single exposed pipe is reasonable. Anything past that is not.

Safe to try

  • Thawing an exposed pipe with a hair dryer.
  • Opening taps to relieve pressure.
  • Wrapping pipes with foam insulation after thaw.
  • Leaving cabinet doors open to warm sink lines.

Stop and call

  • Water is leaking or dripping from a thawed pipe.
  • The frozen section is inside a wall or under the slab.
  • Ceiling is bulging or wet.
  • You cannot reach the main shutoff.

What a Plumber Does at a Freeze Call

A plumber begins by finding the shutoff and pressure-checking the system. We use a thermal camera or moisture meter to find cold spots behind walls without opening them. We thaw the pipe with controlled heat or warm circulation, never an open flame, then inspect every joint for splits.

If a section burst, we cut out the damaged run and replace it with new pipe, then pressure test before closing anything up. For long, damaged runs a repipe may beat patching. When the line between the meter and the house is the problem, we cover that under water service line repair.

Preventing the Next Freeze

Before a hard freeze, disconnect garden hoses and cover outdoor spigots with insulated caps. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls, and let a faucet drip on the coldest night. A thin trickle keeps water moving and relieves pressure between a plug and the tap.

Know where your main shutoff is before you need it. Tag it, and make sure every adult in the house can turn it off. If you leave town during a freeze, set the thermostat no lower than 55 degrees and ask a neighbor to check the house.
