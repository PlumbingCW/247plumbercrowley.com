Water Heater Leaking in Crowley, TX? What to Do Right Now

If your water heater is leaking in Crowley, TX, turn off its power or gas, close the cold water valve above it, and put a pan or towels under the leak. Then find where the water is coming from. A drip from a pipe fitting or the relief valve is often repairable, but water pooling from the bottom of the tank almost always means the tank has failed.

If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. Below are the safe first steps, the common leak spots, a repair-versus-replace table, and what we do once we arrive.

The First Ten Minutes

Speed matters, but so does staying dry and safe. Do these in order.

  1. Stop the heat source.

    For an electric heater, turn off the breaker for the water heater at the panel, and do not touch the panel if the floor is wet. For a gas unit, turn the gas control knob to off. Skip this step if you smell gas and leave instead.

  2. Close the cold supply.

    Find the shutoff valve on the cold pipe above the tank, usually a lever or a round handle, and turn it fully closed. This stops fresh water from feeding the leak.

  3. Catch and divert the water.

    Slide a shallow pan, towels or a wet vac under the drips. If water is running across the floor, move anything valuable out of its path.

  4. Relieve pressure if needed.

    Open a hot faucet in the house to let the tank pressure drop. Do not open the drain valve on a hot tank because the water can scald.

  5. Take photos and call.

    Photograph the unit, the data plate and the wet area. Then call a licensed plumber so they know what to bring.

If You Smell Gas

Leave first, call second

A rotten egg odor near a gas heater means leave the building right away, with everyone and every pet. Call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not flip switches, use a lighter or try to relight a pilot. Call a plumber only after the utility says the house is safe.

Where the Water Is Coming From

The location of the leak usually tells you if you face a repair or a replacement.

Temperature and pressure valve

The T&P valve sits near the top or side with a discharge pipe running down. A drip here can mean excess pressure, thermal expansion or a worn valve. A valve that runs steadily needs attention at once.

Supply and outlet fittings

Water on top of the heater, or around the cold and hot connections, often comes from a loose or corroded fitting. These can often be fixed without touching the tank.

Drain valve

A slow drip at the lower drain valve is common after a flush. Sometimes the washer or the valve itself can be changed.

Tank body

Water from underneath or through the jacket points to a rusted-through tank. There is no safe patch for it, and the unit has reached the end of its life.

Local note

Crowley's Hardness and Your Tank

The City of Crowley 2025 Water Quality Report lists total hardness of 108 to 150 mg/L as CaCO3, which the report states as 6 to 9 grains per gallon. That is moderately hard water.

For your tank, this means minerals settle as sediment at the bottom, form a layer that insulates the water from the burner or element, and can accelerate corrosion in the steel. Flushing a tank each year and checking the anode rod are cheap ways to slow that damage.

Age, leak location and condition decide it. Tank life is typically around 8 to 12 years.

A rusty tank with sediment noise often fails soon, even when the leak looks small.

Why Tanks Leak From the Bottom

Inside every steel tank is a glass lining and a sacrificial anode rod. The rod corrodes first to protect the steel. Once the rod is used up, rust starts at flaws in the lining, and the tank wall thins until water seeps through.

Sediment speeds that process because it traps heat and cooks the lining at the bottom of a gas unit. When you hear popping or rumbling, that is steam bubbling through the mineral layer. If the sound is new and the tank is old, plan for a replacement before the leak starts.

Manufacturer guidelines and the plumbing code both treat a leaking tank as end of life, since a tank that has failed internally cannot be repaired. A leaking fitting or valve is a different, fixable problem.

What You Can Do and What to Leave Alone

Some jobs are safe for a careful homeowner. Others carry real risk.

Safe to do yourself

  • Shut off power or gas and the cold supply.
  • Place a pan and mop up water.
  • Check the discharge pipe for drips.
  • Tighten a hand-loose fitting with a wrench only if the tank is cool.

Leave to a licensed plumber

  • Replace a T&P valve or disconnect piping.
  • Drain a hot tank.
  • Work on gas lines, vents or burners.
  • Relight a pilot on a unit that has leaked.
