Slab Leak Detection Cost in Crowley, TX: What Drives It

Slab leak detection cost in Crowley, TX depends on access, pipe depth, the method used and the number of leaks. We do not post a number, because two houses on the same street can need very different work.

The detection step is separate from the repair. Finding the leak means locating one spot in a run of pipe under your concrete floor, and a licensed plumber uses sound, heat and pressure to do it. Our leak detection service is built around those methods, and you get a clear price before work starts.

Signs the Leak Is Under the Slab

Slab leaks show up first in the bill. Look for these.

A water meter that keeps moving

Turn off every fixture and check the meter dial. If it still spins, water is going somewhere.

A warm spot on the floor

A hot water line leaking under tile or carpet can warm a patch you can feel with bare feet.

The sound of running water

A faint hiss or trickle in a quiet house, with nothing running, points to a pressurized leak.

Damp flooring or a musty smell

Lifted wood planks, soft carpet edges or a sour odor near baseboards can mean moisture coming up.

Why Slabs Leak in This Part of North Texas

Many homes here are built slab-on-grade, with supply lines running through or under the concrete. The clay-heavy soil in this part of North Texas swells when wet and shrinks when dry, so the foundation shifts over the seasons. That movement can rub a pipe against gravel or concrete until it wears through.

Water chemistry plays a part too. Hard water and chloramine disinfectant can wear on copper over decades, and a pinhole in a copper line is a classic slab leak. If your home is older, a pipe check may come up in the same visit.

Detection methods are industry practice rather than a code standard, but the repair that follows must meet the 2021 International Plumbing Code that Crowley uses.

Local note

A Crowley Code Detail That Affects Slab Pipe

Crowley's local amendment to the 2021 International Plumbing Code, Section 305.1, covers metallic piping in the ground. Metal pipe other than cast iron, ductile iron and galvanized steel must not touch corrosive soil directly, and sheathing must be at least 8 mil (0.008 inch).

For a homeowner this matters at repair time. If a leaking buried metal line is replaced or rerouted, the new run has to follow the local rule, so ask the Crowley building department how permits apply before a bigger repair starts.

Source: City of Crowley posted local amendments to the 2021 IPC

What You Can Safely Do Before We Arrive

Do not break the floor yourself. A few quiet checks help us and can limit damage.

  1. Check the meter.

    With all water off, watch the meter. Note whether it moves and write down the reading.

  2. Close the main valve if water is pooling.

    Shut the home's main valve and open one faucet to relieve pressure. Keep people away from wet outlets and panels.

  3. Clear the area.

    Move furniture and rugs off the damp spot so the floor can be inspected.

  4. Take photos.

    Photograph damp walls, warped flooring and your meter. This helps with any insurance claim.

When to call a plumber

  • The meter moves with every fixture off.
  • Water is lifting flooring.
  • You hear running water inside a wall or floor.
  • A bill jumps with no change in use.

What the Detection Visit Looks Like

A licensed plumber starts with the meter and a pressure test on the supply lines. If pressure drops, we isolate zones, like the water heater side, the kitchen side and the bath side, to narrow down which pipe is leaking.

Then comes listening. An acoustic ground microphone sits on the slab while the line is pressurized, and the sound is loudest right over the leak. A thermal camera checks hot lines for heat patterns. When the signals agree, we mark the floor and discuss repair options, including a slab leak repair by tunneling, opening the floor, or rerouting the line through the attic or walls.

Detection that is done carefully avoids unnecessary concrete cuts. That is the value of paying for it.
