How to Clean a Clogged Drain at Home in Crowley, TX

To clean a clogged drain at home in Crowley, TX, start with a plunger, then a hand drain snake. Skip chemical openers. If more than one drain is slow, call a plumber.

Most single-fixture clogs are hair, soap scum, grease or paper within a few feet of the drain. A good plunge or a 25 foot hand snake clears many of them. Our drain cleaning service handles the ones that do not, and this guide shows where to stop.

Clear a Single Drain the Safe Way

Work from the least invasive tool to the most. Stop as soon as the water runs.

  1. Remove standing water and visible debris.

    Bail the sink or tub. Pull out any hair or gunk from the strainer or stopper by hand with gloves.

  2. Plunge with a seal.

    Use a cup plunger on sinks and tubs, and a flange plunger on toilets. Block the overflow opening with a wet rag, then push and pull firmly for 20 seconds.

  3. Snake the trap.

    Feed a small hand snake into the drain until it stops, crank the handle and pull it back. Expect hair or sludge on the tip.

  4. Flush with hot tap water.

    Run hot tap water for a minute. Do not use boiling water, which can soften PVC joints and crack porcelain.

Why Chemical Openers Are a Bad Bet

Especially if the water is backing up

Caustic openers sit in standing water and do not reach a blockage in the main line. They can burn skin and eyes, and they leave dangerous water in the pipe for the next person. Never pour them into a backup.

A plumber working on a drain full of chemicals has to treat the water as hazardous. That slows the job and adds risk. Use a snake or call us instead.

What a Slow Drain Is Telling You

A drain that empties slowly has a partial blockage, and the water will keep taking the path of least resistance until it stops entirely. Slow drains often get worse in steps, not all at once.

Gurgling is air being pulled through a trap because water cannot pass. A smell of sewer gas from a drain can mean a dry trap or a vent problem. Standing water in a tub when the washer drains points to a blockage downstream of both.

Check the pattern before you grab a tool. A single slow sink is usually a trap problem you can fix in ten minutes. A slow tub plus a gurgling toilet on the same branch means the clog is farther down, past anything a plunger can reach.

Drains are built to a plumbing code standard, with traps and vents that keep sewer gas out. If you open a trap and it will not seal again, that is a job for a licensed plumber.

Toilet Clogs Are Their Own Case

A toilet has a built-in trap shaped like an S, and a flange plunger seals into it. Push slowly the first time to push out air, then pull sharply. A closet auger, with its protective sleeve, reaches the bend without scratching the porcelain.

Do not keep flushing a toilet that is rising. The tank will refill the bowl and spill onto the floor. Lift the tank lid and lower the flapper by hand, or close the supply valve at the wall, to stop the water.

DIY or Call: Where the Line Sits

A good test is whether the problem is one fixture or the whole house.

What you can do yourself

  • Plunge one sink, tub or toilet.
  • Clean a P-trap under a sink.
  • Use a hand snake on a short run.
  • Pour a little dish soap and hot tap water down a greasy drain.

When to call a plumber

  • More than one fixture is slow or backed up.
  • Water or sewage rises in the lowest drain.
  • The clog returns within days.
  • A snake hits something solid and will not pass.

What a Plumber Does That a Snake Cannot

A hand snake is a few feet of coiled steel. A professional drain machine runs a cable 50 to 100 feet with cutting heads that chew through roots and grease. It reaches the main line cleanout and beyond.

For stubborn grease, scale or roots, hydro jetting blasts the pipe wall clean with high pressure water. A camera confirms the line is clear and reveals cracks or bellies that cause repeat clogs.

Habits That Keep Drains Moving

A little prevention goes a long way.

  • Use a hair catcher in showers and tubs.
  • Scrape grease into the trash, not the sink.
  • Flush only toilet paper and waste, even if wipes say flushable.
  • Run hot water for 30 seconds after washing dishes.
  • Clean your P-traps and stoppers a couple of times a year.

These habits will not fix an existing clog, but they cut the odds of the next one.
