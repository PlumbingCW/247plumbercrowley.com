How to Repair a Water Heater: Crowley, TX Homeowner Guide

Most water heater trouble comes down to four things. Gas tanks fail at the pilot or igniter, electric tanks at the element or thermostat, and either type clogs with sediment or leaks at a worn valve. Each check takes minutes in a Crowley, TX home.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else. For leaks, shut off power or gas and the cold supply. Otherwise work through this guide, and call (888) 910-8644 if the fix is beyond a safe DIY. We are open 24/7.

If You Smell Gas, Do This First

Leave, then call.

Do not flip switches, light anything, or use a phone inside the house. Walk out, then call your gas utility or 911.

Gas heater work waits until the utility says the leak is cleared. Never try to relight a pilot on a system you suspect is leaking.

What to Do at the Tank Before You Call a Plumber

Each check below needs no repair tools beyond a flashlight and a screwdriver.

Gas: look at the pilot

If the pilot is out and there is no gas smell, follow the instructions on the tank label. If it will not stay lit, stop. A bad thermocouple needs a plumber.

Electric: reset the breaker

Check the double-pole breaker and the red reset button on the upper thermostat. If it trips again, a shorted element is likely. Turn power off before opening any panel.

Check the temperature setting

A setting near 120 degrees is common. Turned down by mistake, a tank feels weak.

Look for a wet floor

Drips from the bottom point to a tank leak, which means replacement. Drips from the relief valve have other causes.

Anode Rods, Elements and Thermostats Explained

The anode rod is a magnesium or aluminum rod screwed into the top of the tank. It corrodes on purpose so the steel tank does not. Once the rod is eaten down to the core wire, the tank starts to rust, and a rotten egg smell often shows up first.

On electric tanks, two screw-in elements heat the water, each controlled by its own thermostat. If the upper element fails, you get no hot water. If the lower one fails, you get a short supply. A multimeter shows continuity, but turn the power off at the breaker before opening any access panel.

A common mistake is turning the thermostat way up to fix a weak tank. Water above 120 degrees raises scald risk without fixing the underlying failure. If the temperature setting was never the problem, adjust it back and look elsewhere.

Water heater safety follows manufacturer instructions and plumbing code standards. According to those guidelines, never remove or plug a T&P valve, and leave gas appliance work to a licensed plumber.

Local note

What the Crowley Water Report Says

The City of Crowley 2025 Water Quality Report lists chloramine as the disinfectant, with a 2025 average of 2.56 ppm, and a pH range of 7.8 to 8.5. The water is hard at 6 to 9 grains per gallon in the same report.

For your tank, that mix is why sediment builds up and why the anode rod, a sacrificial metal rod inside, wears out. Hard water leaves scale on elements and the tank floor. If your tank is near the end of the typical 8 to 12 year life, a failing anode is a good reason to plan for replacement rather than another repair.

Flush the Tank to Clear Sediment

A yearly flush clears the scale that causes rumbling and weak output. It takes about an hour.

  1. Turn off power or gas.

    Set the breaker off or the gas control to pilot. Let the water cool for a few hours so a hose does not scald you.

  2. Connect a hose.

    Attach a garden hose to the drain valve and run it to a floor drain or outdoors, downhill from the heater.

  3. Open a hot tap and the drain.

    Open a hot faucet upstairs to break the vacuum, then open the drain valve. Let water run until it clears.

  4. Close up and refill.

    Close the drain valve, remove the hose, and refill with the cold supply open and a hot tap running until air stops spitting.

  5. Restore power or gas.

    Only turn power on after the tank is full. Turning an electric element on a dry tank burns it out.

Repair It or Replace It

Age and the failure type decide the answer.

Repair makes sense

  • The tank is under about 8 years old.
  • The failed part is an element, thermostat, igniter or valve.
  • There is no leak from the tank shell.
  • Hot water ran well until recently.

Replace makes sense

  • The tank leaks from the body or bottom.
  • It is past roughly 10 to 12 years.
  • Several parts have failed in a short time.
  • You want to move to a tankless unit.
