Sewer Line Repair or Replacement for Crowley, TX Homes

Whether a Crowley, TX sewer line needs repair or full replacement depends on what the camera shows. A single cracked joint calls for a spot repair. A long run of broken or bellied pipe usually points to lining or replacement.

You should not decide before you see the inside of the pipe. A sewer camera inspection shows the cracks, offsets and root intrusion, and then the choice becomes a matter of how much pipe is damaged and what it takes to reach it.

Spot Repair, Lining or Replacement

Most jobs fall into one of three buckets. This table shows where each fits.

OptionBest whenTrade-off
Spot repair.One short section is cracked, offset or root-damaged.Leaves the rest of the old pipe in place.
Pipe lining.The pipe is mostly sound but has several cracks or joint leaks.Needs a pipe with a clear path and decent shape.
Full replacement.The pipe is collapsed, bellied or failing along its length.Most digging and restoration.
Pipe bursting.The old line can be broken and replaced in place.Needs access pits at both ends.

A licensed plumber should show you the camera video before recommending any of these.

Signs the Line Is Failing

A damaged sewer pipe rarely fails all at once. Watch for these.

Repeated clogs

The same drain backs up every few weeks, even after cleaning.

Gurgling drains

A toilet that bubbles when the washer drains can mean air trapped by a blockage.

Wet or sunken yard patches

A soggy, smelly spot over the sewer path suggests a break.

Sewer smell indoors or outdoors

Gas from a cracked line can reach the crawl space, garage or yard.

What the Camera Is Looking For

A small camera on a flexible rod runs the full length of the line, from the cleanout to the tie-in at the main. The video records the distance to every defect, so we can mark the spot above ground.

We look for cracked or broken pipe, joints pulled apart, roots growing through gaps, and a belly where water sits instead of draining. Each tells us a little about the cause. A belly often traces to settled or poorly compacted soil, while roots mean a gap in a joint.

Why the cause matters for the repair

Fixing a crack in a pipe sitting in shifting clay without fixing the support below it can mean the same failure in a few years. The repair has to address the bedding as well as the break.

Local note

Crowley's Local Rules for Buried Sewer Pipe

Crowley's local amendments to the 2021 International Plumbing Code set two requirements that affect a sewer dig. Section 305.4.1 puts building sewers at least 12 inches below grade, and Section 306.2.4 requires underground plastic drain piping to sit in 4 inches of granular bedding compacted to 85 percent standard proctor.

For you, that means a proper repair is more than swapping pipe. The trench depth, the bedding and the compaction all count, and they matter for a reason: weak bedding is how bellies form. Ask the Crowley building department how permits and inspections apply to your job.

Source: City of Crowley posted local amendments to the 2021 IPC

How a Sewer Repair Job Usually Goes

Every job differs, but the path is familiar.

  1. Camera and locate.

    We run the camera, mark the depth and the defect, and show you the video.

  2. Agree the plan and the price.

    You get a clear price for the chosen option before any digging or lining starts.

  3. Prepare and protect.

    Utilities are located, the area is marked, and plants and hardscape are protected where we can.

  4. Repair or replace.

    We dig, line or burst the pipe, set it on proper bedding, and connect to the existing line.

  5. Test and close up.

    The line is tested and re-scoped, then backfilled, compacted and the surface restored.

Lining and Bursting, Honestly

Lining pushes a resin-soaked tube into the old pipe and cures it into a new inner wall. It avoids a long trench, but the old pipe must be clear and round enough to accept it. Heavy roots or a collapsed section need to be cleared or fixed first.

Pipe bursting pulls a new plastic pipe through the old path while breaking the old one outward. It needs pits at each end instead of a continuous trench. It does not suit every soil or every pipe, and a belly in the old line can carry over unless the path is corrected.

We tell you plainly which options fit your camera video. If digging is the only sound answer, we say so.

What You Can Do and What to Leave to a Plumber

Sewer work involves gas, bacteria and deep digging, so the split is firm.

What you can do yourself

  • Stop running water if the line backs up.
  • Find your cleanout and keep it clear.
  • Note where odors and wet spots appear.
  • Keep wipes and grease out of drains.

When to call a plumber

  • Sewage is backing into a tub or floor drain.
  • The yard is sinking over the sewer path.
  • Clogs keep coming back.
  • You are about to dig or buy a house.

A Dig Near Older Trees and Newer Fences

Tree roots and fences complicate any trench. A large tree over the line may make lining a better fit than digging, and a fence or deck may need partial removal if a dig is the only way.

Our crews cover Crowley and neighboring towns. Homeowners in Burleson and Everman face similar conditions, with clay soil shifting seasonally. If you need sewer line repair, we are open 24/7 and offer same-day appointments when available.

Buying a Home? Scope the Line First

A sewer inspection before closing costs little next to a surprise failure. The video tells you whether the line is clear, how it is bedded and whether roots are working into the joints.

If you are choosing between houses, a clean camera report is a useful tiebreaker. It also gives you a baseline to compare against if the line starts acting up later.
