Under Sink Garbage Disposal Installation in Crowley, TX

A garbage disposal installation in a Crowley, TX kitchen takes a few hours. The job is five steps: kill power, mount the unit, connect the drain, open the dishwasher knockout, and test for leaks. The wiring decides who should do it. A plug-in unit is a reasonable weekend project, and a hardwired one is not.

If you can turn off a breaker, handle a plumber's putty and a screwdriver, and own a bucket, the swap is doable. If the sink drain is corroded, the cabinet has no outlet or the cabinet floor is wet, hand it to a licensed plumber. Here is what the job involves.

Parts and Tools to Have on the Counter

Buy the pieces before you open the cabinet. A half-finished install with no water in the kitchen is no fun on a Sunday evening.

  • A disposal rated 1/3, 1/2 or 3/4 horsepower, with a mounting kit included.
  • Plumber's putty or the gasket that comes in the box.
  • A new discharge elbow and a rubber gasket, usually supplied with the unit.
  • A power cord kit for a plug-in setup, or a cable clamp and wire nuts for hardwired.
  • A new 1-1/2 inch tailpiece and trap adapter if the old ones are cracked.
  • Slip-joint pliers, a screwdriver, a hammer and a short length of wood for the knockout.
  • A bucket, rags and a flashlight.

Check the brand's instructions for the exact mounting style. Most modern units use a three-bolt ring that locks onto the sink flange with a half-turn.

Plug-In or Hardwired: Choosing the Electrical Setup

Your cabinet and your comfort with wiring decide this more than the disposal itself.

Plug-in cord

  • Needs a grounded outlet inside the sink cabinet, usually switched from the wall.
  • Easier to remove for service because you just unplug it.
  • Check that the cord rating matches the unit and that the outlet is not under a leaky pipe.

Hardwired

  • Connects to a cable through the cabinet wall with a clamp and wire nuts.
  • Cleaner look and no outlet to get wet.
  • Best left to a licensed plumber or electrician unless you already know how to work with live circuits.

Installing the Unit, in Order

Work with the power off and the kitchen free of people. Here is the sequence a plumber follows.

  1. Cut power and clear the cabinet.

    Turn off the breaker for the disposal circuit and confirm with a tester. Unplug or disconnect the old unit, support it with one hand, and twist it off its mounting ring. Disconnect the drain trap and dishwasher hose, and keep a bucket under the trap.

  2. Set the flange.

    Remove the old mounting assembly from the sink opening, clean the old putty off, and press a rope of fresh putty under the new flange lip. Seat it from above and tighten the mounting ring from below until putty squeezes out evenly. Wipe the extra putty away.

  3. Open the dishwasher knockout.

    If a dishwasher drains into the unit, a plastic plug blocks the inlet. Set a screwdriver on the plug and tap it with a hammer until it drops inside, then fish it out. Forgetting this step causes the dishwasher to back up.

  4. Hang the disposal and connect the drain.

    Lift the unit onto the mounting ring, line up the tabs and rotate it until it locks. Attach the discharge elbow with its gasket, then connect the elbow to the trap with slip joints. The trap should slope toward the wall drain, not away from it.

  5. Wire and test.

    Connect the cord or cable, secure the cover plate and restore power. Run cold water, flip the switch and watch every joint with a flashlight and a dry paper towel for drips.

Where New Installs Go Wrong

Most callbacks trace to a small set of mistakes.

Missed knockout

The dishwasher drains into a blocked inlet and water sits in the tub after every cycle.

Crooked mount

If the three-bolt ring is not tightened evenly, the gasket leaks at the flange and drips down the cabinet.

Wrong trap slope

A trap that tilts the wrong way holds standing water that can smell and slow the drain.

Short dishwasher hose loop

The dishwasher hose should rise as high as the cabinet allows before dropping to the disposal. A low hose lets gray water flow back into the dishwasher.

When to Stop and Call a Plumber

Stop if the supply or drain connections look corroded, if the old flange will not release or if there is water under the cabinet. A soft cabinet floor is a sign that the leak has run for a while. Do not wire a disposal if the circuit is shared with other loads or you are unsure of the breaker label.

Keep your hands out of the disposal at all times. If it hums but will not spin, switch the power off and use the hex socket on the bottom to turn it by hand. That is a fix for a jam, not a reason to reach in. For a unit that has failed, see our garbage disposal repair page for the options.

A disposal install touches the drain and the electrical supply, so it is governed by the plumbing code and the electrical code. According to the manufacturer instructions, a plug-in or hardwired connection must match the unit.
