The Problems Everman Callers Describe

Most calls begin with one of these. Each points at a different part of the system.

Gurgling toilets and a wet floor drain

Air and water are fighting for a way out of the main line. The blockage is usually past the last branch, so one clear fixture does not mean the house is fine.

Slow drains in more than one room

A single slow sink is a trap or a branch. Two or three at once means the shared line is narrowing, often from grease or roots.

Sewer smell in the yard

A sulfur odor near the cleanout can mean a cracked or offset section of pipe. A camera shows the break, so nobody has to guess and dig.

Water pressure dropping

Pressure falls for house-side reasons like a failing regulator or a leak, and for city-side reasons. We help sort out which one before you pay for a repair.

A Clog You Can Try First Versus a Clog to Hand Over

Not every stoppage needs a truck. Here is where the line sits.

Try these yourself

  • Use a cup plunger on a single sink or tub with the overflow plugged.
  • Pour hot, not boiling, tap water after dish grease softens.
  • Pull the hair out of a shower strainer by hand or with a drain tool.

Stop and call a plumber

  • Two fixtures back up together, or a toilet bubbles when the washer drains.
  • Water appears at a floor drain or cleanout.
  • A snake meets a hard stop it cannot pass.
  • Any chemical opener was already poured, because it burns skin and ruins cables.

Sewer Line Repair When Cleaning Is Not Enough

If a cleared line clogs again within weeks, a cable only treated the symptom. A sewer camera shows roots, a sag where water pools, or a crack, and the picture decides between sewer line cleaning and a repair. Spot repairs, lining and full replacement each suit different damage.

Permit rules for digging vary, so we ask the city building department before any trench work. Our sewer line repair page explains the options and the trade-offs, and hydro jetting often clears the grease that cabling leaves behind.

Everman's own water department sets the rules for its water system, so ask them about connection and meter standards. For the plumbing inside the house, the 2021 International Plumbing Code is the model code most Texas cities adopt.

Before We Arrive in Everman

When water is already on a floor, how you spend the next few minutes matters. These steps keep a small problem small.

Lay off the water.

Do not run the washer, dishwasher or showers. In a backup, every drop you add heads for the lowest opening in the house.

Shut the main if water is spilling.

Turn the main valve clockwise at the meter box or the house. If you cannot find or turn it, say so on the phone and we will talk you through it.

Keep people and pets back.

Sewage carries germs, so keep children and animals out of the wet area. Never pour bleach or drain opener into a backup.

Aquifer Wells, Chloramine and What Comes Out of the Tap

Everman monitors groundwater wells in the Paluxy and Twin Mountains aquifers, and the department uses chloramine and adds fluoride. That differs from Crowley and Burleson, which buy treated surface water from Fort Worth. The city page gives no hardness figure, so ask the water department for a current report before buying a softener.

Chloramine is rougher on old rubber parts than plain chlorine, so toilet flappers and some supply connectors may wear sooner. If a toilet runs after a few years, the flapper is a cheap first check. Our toilet repair page covers that and the fill valve.

The Everman Parkway Main and Your Water Pressure

The city is working with Fort Worth on a 16-inch transmission main on Everman Parkway. A project like that can mean shutoffs and flushing near the work. If your pressure dips or the water looks cloudy, check the neighbors and call the water department before assuming your pipes failed.

If only your house is affected, the cause is on your side of the meter, such as a stuck regulator or a slow leak. A running meter with every fixture off points to a hidden leak, and leak detection can find it without opening a wall.
