Why Downtown Crowley Plumbing Is Different

Crowley began near Deer Creek around 1848. When the Gulf, Colorado and Santa Fe Railway built its pens and tracks, the settlement moved about a mile west to the site of present-day downtown. The city center is still the crossroads of FM 1187 and FM 731, and the blocks around it are the older core of a city that grew 40.8 percent between 2010 and 2020.

Many Crowley homes are comparatively recent next to the downtown core, but the sources we checked do not date any individual house. A home built before the mid-1990s may still have galvanized steel supply pipe, cast iron drains or gray polybutylene supply lines, and each fails in its own way.

Problems Older Lines Tend to Show

A main drain that clogs again and again

Roots find joints in old clay and cast iron lines. A cable clears the stoppage but not the cause, so a sewer camera inspection shows what is really in the pipe.

Weak pressure at one fixture

Galvanized steel narrows from the inside as mineral scale builds. If one tap runs weak while the rest are fine, the supply branch may be the problem, which is where repiping enters the conversation.

A wet patch between the meter and the house

Damp ground or a meter that keeps spinning with every tap off suggests a service line leak. Shut off the main valve and call before the soil washes out.

Ask the City Before the Ground Is Opened

City of Crowley Community Development is at 201 E. Main Street and takes permit questions at 817-297-2201, extension 3000. Digging up a sewer or water line is the kind of work where the permit question belongs at the start, so ask before a trench is cut. Crowley has adopted the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and the city posts its local amendments.

For a leak on the street side of your meter, Public Works handles water distribution and sewer collection. Ask where the city's side of the line ends, since the city pages do not say.
