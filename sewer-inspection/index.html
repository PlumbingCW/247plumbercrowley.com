Sewer Inspection in Crowley, TX

A sewer inspection in Crowley, TX puts a waterproof camera inside your main drain line so you can see the pipe instead of guessing. We use it for slow drains, repeat clogs, home purchases, and any job where someone plans to dig. You schedule it by calling our 24/7 line, and a plumber books the visit.

The camera reaches from a cleanout or a pulled toilet toward the city connection, recording as it goes. We read what it shows, mark the pipe's path above ground, and give you the findings in plain English. That report can save you from buying a failing line or from digging in the wrong place.

What the Camera Shows You

The picture on the screen replaces a lot of speculation. These are the findings that come up most.

Roots

Fine roots enter through joints and thicken into a mass that catches paper and grease. Roots mean the pipe has a gap, so cutting them clears the line for a while but does not seal it.

Bellies and sags

A belly is a low spot that holds water and waste. It often comes from soil settling under the pipe, and you can spot one when the camera shows a standing pool that never drains.

Cracks and offsets

Joints that have slid apart or a pipe that has split along its length show up as dark gaps or a ragged edge. These are the defects that eventually need sewer line repair.

Grease and scale

Coating on the pipe wall narrows the passage over time. If the pipe is otherwise sound, a cleaning pass such as hydro jetting often solves it.

Belly-free, clean pipe

Sometimes the answer is good news. A smooth, continuous line with no sags tells you the clog was local, such as a toilet trap or a branch drain.

Collapsed or crushed sections

A collapse can stop the camera cold. Where it stops, we note the distance and locate it from above so you know how far from the house the problem sits.

Why Clay Soil Matters Underground

In this part of North Texas the clay soil swells when wet and shrinks when dry. That seasonal movement pushes and pulls on buried pipe, and over many cycles a line can sag, separate at a joint, or crack. It does not mean every line fails, but it explains why a camera look is worth it on any older drain and on any line with a history of backups.

Crowley's local plumbing amendments address this directly. They require underground plastic drain piping to sit on 4 inches of granular bedding with 85 percent standard proctor compaction, and building sewers at least 12 inches below grade. Those rules show the city treats soil support as part of a good install, and they guide what we look for when a line has settled.

How an Inspection Visit Works

Most visits take a short stretch of the day, and you do not need to be home for all of it.

  1. We find the access point.

    A cleanout outside the house is the best entry. If there is none, we pull a toilet or reach the line through a roof vent, then run a short test flush to confirm which way water flows.

  2. The camera goes in.

    A flexible rod carries a sealed camera head with lights. As it advances, a counter tracks the distance in feet and we record the whole run.

  3. We locate and mark.

    A transmitter in the camera head sends a signal we trace from above. We mark the line's path and depth on the yard or driveway so you know where it runs.

  4. You get the findings.

    We show you the recording, explain what each defect means, and say plainly whether it needs cleaning, repair, or nothing at all. You decide what comes next.

Before You Dig: Locating the Line First

If you plan to add a fence, pool, or patio, the sewer line location matters. Digging into a sewer line is messy and expensive to fix. A camera with a locator marks where the pipe runs and how deep, so contractors can plan around it.

The depth also tells you something. Crowley's amendments set a minimum depth of 12 inches below grade for building sewers, so a shallow reading can mean the pipe was installed outside that rule or has been disturbed by erosion. Either way, a defect there is much cheaper to find now than with a trench open.

City of Crowley Inspections and Your Sewer Work

If a sewer repair or replacement needs a city inspection, requests go through the City of Crowley Community Development office at 817-297-2201, ext. 3020. The permits office is at ext. 3000, open 7:30 to 5:30 Monday through Thursday and 7:30 to 11:30 on Friday morning. Permit rules vary, so ask the Crowley building department whether your project needs one.

Camera inspections themselves are a look, not a repair. Plan on calling the city only if your findings lead to digging or replacing pipe.

When to Schedule a Sewer Camera Inspection

Not every drain problem needs a camera. These situations do.

  • Two or more drains back up at the same time.
  • A clog returns weeks after a cleaning.
  • A gurgle or sewer smell in a bathroom.
  • Wet, sunken ground along the sewer path in the yard.
  • You are buying or selling a home.
  • You plan to build or excavate near the line.

For clogs that need clearing right now, our drain cleaning team can step in, and the camera helps confirm the line is clear afterward.
