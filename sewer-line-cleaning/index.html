Main Line Backup: Is It Your Pipe or the City's?

This is the first question we ask on the phone, because it decides who should be called and whether a plumber visit will help.

  1. Check the other drains.

    Run a hot water tap upstairs for a minute. If the lowest tub or floor drain gurgles or rises, the main line is blocked. If only one fixture is slow, the problem is a branch line and a smaller clog.

  2. Ask the neighbors.

    If houses on your street have the same trouble at the same time, the city main may be blocked. Do not guess. Call Public Works at 817-297-2201 during business hours.

  3. Look at the street.

    Sewage flowing out of a manhole cover is a city main problem. After-hours wastewater backup reports go to Police Dispatch at 817-297-2276, and you should call them before you call us.

  4. Find your cleanout.

    Most homes have a capped 3 or 4 inch pipe near the foundation or the property line. If sewage is standing in that pipe, the blockage sits between the cleanout and the city, or in the line downstream of it. Do not open a cleanout with sewage standing in it.

What Clogs a Crowley Main Line

Grease is the usual culprit. Cooking fat goes down warm and liquid, then cools and sticks to the pipe wall, narrowing a 4 inch line a little more every month. Disposable wipes labeled flushable do not break down, and they catch on roots and joint offsets.

Roots come second. Fine roots enter a joint, grow into a mat that works like a net, and hold everything that passes. In clay-heavy North Texas soil that swells and shrinks with the weather, joints shift, and each shift can give roots a new entry point.

Cleaning does not change the pipe, but the standards behind it matter. According to Crowley's local amendment, underground plastic drain pipe is laid on 4 inches of granular bedding, which is why a gentle cable beats an aggressive one on older runs.

Cabling or Jetting: Matching the Tool to the Clog

Both tools clear a line. They work differently, and the right choice depends on what is in the pipe.

Cable machine (snake)A rotating steel cable with a cutter head bores through the blockage.Roots, soft clogs and fast access to a stopped line.
Hydro jettingA nozzle sends water at high pressure to scour the pipe wall.Grease buildup, scale and a full clean-out of the line.

How a Cleaning Visit Goes

We locate the cleanout and place a drop cloth. Then we run a cable machine sized to the pipe, a 3/4 inch to 1 1/2 inch cable for a typical home main, until the line flows. On roots we use a cutter head, and on grease we often follow with a jetting pass so the pipe wall is actually clean.

After the line is open we run the camera. A clean line with no damage ends the visit. If the footage shows a crack, belly or offset, we show you the images and explain the next step, which may involve sewer line repair. If you would like a stand-alone look at your line, our sewer inspection visits cover that.

Keeping the Line Open Afterward

A cleaned line that gets the same abuse will clog again. These habits make the difference.

  • Wipe grease from pans.Scrape fat into a can and throw it in the trash instead of rinsing it down the sink.
  • Only toilet paper in the toilet.No wipes, no cotton balls, no feminine products, even if the package says flushable.
  • Know where your cleanout is.A cleanout lets us clear the line without pulling a toilet.
  • Watch tree roots.If a tree stands over the line, schedule a camera check every year or two.
  • Call after the second clog.A line that clogs twice in a few months usually has a physical defect that cleaning alone will not fix.
