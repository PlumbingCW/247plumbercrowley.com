Whole House Repiping in Crowley, TX

Whole house repiping in Crowley, TX means replacing the water supply pipes throughout a home, usually because they leak, corrode, or deliver weak, discolored water. Our line runs 24/7, and a plumber can come out for a look. We tell you honestly whether you need a full repipe or a targeted repair.

Most pipes never need replacing. When one does, the question is whether it is a one-spot failure or a sign the whole system has reached its limit. This page explains how to tell, which materials cause trouble, and how a repipe works.

Signs the Pipes Are Wearing Out

One leak is bad luck. These patterns usually mean the system itself is the problem.

Repeated pinhole leaks

A second or third pinhole in the same line suggests the pipe wall is thinning. Patching each one costs more over time than replacing the run.

Rusty or brown water

Rust-colored water from the hot or cold tap, especially first thing in the morning, points to corroding steel. It can clear after running a while, but it keeps returning.

Low pressure everywhere

Old galvanized pipe fills with mineral and rust inside, shrinking the opening. You may see a shower that slowly turns into a trickle.

Visible green or white crust

Crusty buildup at fittings and joints signals active seepage. It often shows up under sinks and near the water heater.

Wet spots with no source

A damp wall, stain, or high water bill with nothing obvious wrong needs leak detection first, and may lead to a repipe if the lines are failing.

Pipes flagged at inspection

A home inspector may note a known problem material during a purchase. That is a good time to plan the repipe on your terms.

The Materials That Cause Trouble

This is general trade knowledge, not a claim about any specific Crowley home. Homes built before the mid-1990s may still have polybutylene, the gray plastic supply pipe used in many US houses until that time. It can become brittle and fail at fittings, which is why plumbers recommend replacing it when found.

Older homes in any city may have galvanized steel, which rusts from the inside and slows flow, or cast iron drain stacks. Newer construction commonly uses copper, CPVC, or PEX, and each has its own failure patterns. Finding out what you have is step one, and we can identify it quickly during a visit.

A repipe is code work. Crowley has adopted the 2021 International Plumbing Code, and the state's licensing standard means you can verify any plumber in the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners public license search before work starts.

Pipe Types Compared

Here is how the common supply pipe materials behave over time.

MaterialCommon troubleRepipe note
Polybutylene (gray).Brittle fittings, sudden failure.Replace the whole supply system.
Galvanized steel.Internal rust, weak flow, stains.Replace, since it only gets worse.
Copper.Pinholes, joint leaks, pitting.Spot repair if isolated, repipe if widespread.
CPVC.Brittle with age, cracks at fittings.Repair or replace sections, depending on condition.
PEX.Rare issues, mostly at fittings.Often the material we replace with.

Pipe type is only one factor. Water pressure, installation quality, and soil contact all affect how long a line lasts.

What the Water Report Says About Copper

The City of Crowley 2025 Water Quality Report lists the copper 90th percentile at 0.164 ppm against the 1.3 ppm action level. That means the sampled homes were well below the limit set for copper in drinking water. For you, a leak or a blue-green stain in your house is more likely a local pipe issue than a citywide problem.

Corrosion still happens at the fitting level. If copper pinholes keep recurring, we look at velocity, joint quality, and any contact between copper and concrete or soil, rather than blaming the water.

Spot Repair or Whole House Repipe

The right answer depends on the pipe, the damage, and how long you plan to stay.

Repair a section when

  • One leak in an otherwise healthy line.
  • The pipe is copper or PEX in good shape.
  • The damage came from a single event, such as a nail or a freeze.
  • You can reach the section easily.
  • Water tests and pressure checks look normal.

Repipe when

  • Leaks keep returning in different spots.
  • The home has polybutylene or heavily corroded galvanized.
  • Water turns brown or pressure keeps falling.
  • You are remodeling and walls are already open.
  • Repair costs are catching up to replacement.

How a Repipe Works

We plan the job to keep disruption low, and we explain each step before work starts.

  1. Inspect and plan.

    We trace the existing supply lines, check pressure with a gauge, and decide on a route for new pipe. Then we lay out a plan with a clear price.

  2. Protect the house.

    We cover floors and furniture, and shut off the water at the main. Most repipes are done over a day or two, though larger homes take longer.

  3. Open access points.

    We cut small openings in drywall or work through attics and crawlspaces where possible. We aim to run new lines through paths that need the least patching.

  4. Install, test, and reconnect.

    New lines go in with proper support and fittings, and we pressure-test before closing anything up. Then we reconnect fixtures, the water heater, and shutoff valves.

Copper Repair and CPVC Replacement

Many calls we get are not full repipes. A copper line with one pinhole or a failed joint can often be cut out and replaced with a new section. We use press fittings or sweated joints, and we check nearby pipe for the same wear.

CPVC is common in newer homes and is usually fine, but it can crack at fittings or in sunlit areas as it ages. If a section fails, we replace it and look at the surrounding runs. When damage is sudden, such as a cracked line flooding a room, our burst pipe repair team can step in.

Newer Homes Are Not Immune

Crowley's population grew 40.8 percent from 12,838 in 2010 to 18,070 in 2020, which means many homes here are comparatively recent. Newer does not mean problem-free. Poor installation, high pressure, or a defective fitting can lead to leaks regardless of age. We make no assumptions about your home. We inspect what is there.

If your service line from the meter to the house is the issue, it is a separate job covered under water service line repair. And for leaks under floors, see slab leak repair.

Check Any Plumber's License

Before a big job, ask the company for its Responsible Master Plumber and check that number in the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners Public License Search. Master Plumber numbers start with M. The search also shows whether a Certificate of Insurance is on file, which matters on a job this size.
