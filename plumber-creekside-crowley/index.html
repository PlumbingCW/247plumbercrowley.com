Reading the Ground Under a Creekside Home

In this part of North Texas, clay-heavy soil swells when it is wet and shrinks when it is dry, and slab-on-grade foundations are common. A slab can shift by small amounts through the seasons. A copper or PEX line that runs through or under it can rub, kink or crack as the ground moves.

We have not verified the foundation type for every Creekside home, so treat that as a likely pattern, not a fact about your address. If your house sits on a slab, hidden leaks usually announce themselves through a spinning meter, a warm patch on the floor or a water bill that jumps with no change in habits.

How a Hidden Leak Gets Traced

A good diagnosis finds the leak without opening the whole floor.

  1. Confirm the leak with the meter.

    Shut off every fixture. If the dial still turns, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and the taps.

  2. Isolate the line.

    Closing individual shutoffs shows whether the leak sits on the supply side, the water heater or an irrigation line.

  3. Pinpoint it.

    Leak detection uses listening gear and tracer methods to find the spot within inches, so the opening can be small.

  4. Choose the fix.

    A slab leak repair may go through the floor, reroute the line or replace a section. Each carries different disruption, so ask for options.

Drains That Slow Down Before They Stop

A single sink that gurgles is a trap or a branch. A house where the tub, the washer and a toilet all react at once points to the shared line. In that case a drain cleaning visit clears the immediate blockage, and a longer-term fix depends on what the camera finds.

If a line fills with grease or roots again and again, sewer line cleaning by jetting scours the pipe walls. Crowley has adopted the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), so ask that new drain work follows it.
